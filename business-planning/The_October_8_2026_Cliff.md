# The October 8, 2026 Cliff

## The Single Sentence

**On October 8, 2026, the Class B majority holder gains the unilateral right to force a sale of Class at any price they choose — including a price that wipes out Russell's Class E units entirely — and Russell has no legal right to refuse.**

That date is 139 days from today. Everything else in the strategy follows from this one fact.

This document explains why.

---

## How Russell Gets Paid When Class Sells

Russell has two distinct compensation streams from the October 2025 recap. They pay differently, vest differently, and get protected differently. Understanding the difference matters because each one cares about the sale price in a different way.

### Stream 1: The Bonus Agreement (the floor)

Russell holds a **2.25% Designated Percentage** under a separate Bonus Agreement tied to the recap. This is NOT equity. It's a contractual obligation Class owes Russell, paid in cash at a sale, calculated as:

> 2.25% × cash flowing to Barclays under Section 15.4(a)(ii), capped at the Senior Secured Debt amount (~$30M)

In plain English: when Class sells, the lawyers add up all the proceeds, take out wind-up expenses, and then start paying Barclays back the senior debt. At the same time they're paying Barclays, they're also paying out the Bonus Agreement holders. The Bonus Agreement is paid **in parallel with the debt, not after it.** Russell gets his 2.25% slice of every dollar going to Barclays, up to a maximum payout of about **$675K** (when Barclays has been fully repaid the $30M).

This is Russell's floor. Even at a fire-sale price, even at $20M, this stream pays *something* — proportional to how much of the debt got cleared.

### Stream 2: The 180 Class E Common Units (the upside)

Russell also holds 180 Class E Common Units out of 1,000 authorized — meaning he has 18% of the Class E pool. The Class E pool collectively gets 12.50% of sale proceeds via the waterfall in Section 15.4(a)(iv) and (a)(v). So Russell's effective slice is:

> 12.50% × 18% = **2.25% of certain tranches of sale proceeds**

The catch: Class E only sees money **after** debt + Bonus + wind-up expenses are fully paid. The senior secured debt is $31.4M. With the Bonus Agreement consuming 14% of every dollar going to debt, the total "debt + Bonus" obligation absorbs the first **~$36.5M** of any transaction. Below that, Class E gets $0.

Above $36.5M, Russell starts earning roughly 2.25% of every incremental dollar. Above $75M, the math shifts slightly because Class A gets a preferred return, but Russell's marginal rate stays around 2.25%.

### The Combined Picture

| Sale Price | Bonus (Stream 1) | Class E (Stream 2) | **Russell Total** | What Happens to Barclays |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $20M | ~$368K | $0 | **~$368K** | Debt underwater ~$15M (Barclays vetoes) |
| $30M | ~$551K | $0 | **~$551K** | Debt underwater ~$7M (Barclays vetoes) |

| $150M | $675K | ~$2.45M | **~$3.13M** | Debt + premium equity |

**The Stream 1 floor caps at $675K. Everything above that depends on Stream 2 — and Stream 2 is what's exposed to the drag-along cliff.**

## What "Drag-Along" Means in Plain English

A drag-along right is a provision in equity agreements that lets a majority holder force minority holders to participate in a sale on the same terms. If Class B (the majority economic holder) finds a buyer and decides to sell, the drag-along right means Russell — and every other Class A, C, D, E member — has to sell too, at the same price, with no veto.

Drag-along rights exist because acquirers usually want to buy 100% of a company, not 78%. Without a drag, a minority holder could refuse to sell and block the deal. So venture-backed and PE-backed companies almost always have drag rights baked into their operating agreements.

**Class's drag-along is in Section 11.8 of the Operating Agreement.** It says: any group of Members holding a majority of Class B Common Units can require all other Members to sell their Units to a Third Party Purchaser, on the same terms.

That much is standard. The interesting part is what comes next.

## The $100M Floor and the One-Year Window

Section 11.8(a), read carefully:

> "...such right of the Drag-Along Members to require the other Members to sell all of their Units in connection with such Drag-Along Sale shall not apply unless the gross proceeds in such transaction is at least $100 million."

And then this:

> "...prior to the first anniversary of the date of this Agreement..."

The Operating Agreement was effective **October 8, 2025**. The first anniversary is **October 8, 2026**.

So the language reads as: for the first year of the agreement, the drag-along right only applies if gross proceeds are at least $100M. After the first year — after October 8, 2026 — that floor disappears. Class B can drag everyone at *any* price.

This is unusual. Most drag rights either have no minimum or have a permanent minimum. The structure here is specifically a one-year floor that expires. Whoever drafted this — likely Holdco's counsel — built in a one-year period where the Class B holder would have to either get a premium price ($100M+) or sit on the asset before they could force a low-ball outcome on the minority.

That one year is almost half over.

**Today is May 22, 2026. The floor expires October 8, 2026. We have 139 days.**

## What Changes on October 8, 2026

To make the impact concrete, imagine two scenarios. Same buyer. Same sale price. Different date.

### Scenario A — Sale closes October 7, 2026 at $30M

Class B finds a strategic buyer willing to pay $30M cash. They want to take the deal. Russell does NOT want to take the deal because at $30M:
- Russell gets ~$551K (Bonus only, Class E = $0)
- Barclays takes a $7M debt haircut

Under the drag-along, Class B tries to force the deal. **But on October 7, the drag-along still requires $100M minimum. $30M doesn't meet the threshold. Russell can refuse to sell, and his refusal blocks the deal** (assuming the buyer needs 100% — which they almost always do).

What happens next? Class B has three real options:
1. Find a better buyer at $100M+
2. Wait until October 8, 2026 and force the $30M deal then
3. Try to ROFR-purchase Russell's units individually (Section 11.6 mechanics)

In any of those scenarios, Russell has leverage. The minority holder veto is real.

### Scenario B — Sale closes October 9, 2026 at $30M

Same deal. Same buyer. Same $30M. Different date by 48 hours.

The drag-along $100M floor expired the day before. Class B serves a Drag-Along Notice on Russell. Russell has no legal grounds to refuse. He has to:
- Sign the purchase agreement
- Sign customary reps and warranties
- Pay his pro-rata share of transaction costs
- Accept his $551K and walk away

Russell's Class E units are gone for $0. Whatever vested Bonus Agreement amount he has is paid (proportional to debt repayment under 15.4(a)(ii)). The COO chapter closes with about half a year of base salary worth of equity proceeds, against the $1-2M+ outcome that would have been available at a $100M+ sale.

**Same buyer. Same offer. Two-day difference in calendar. About $1.5M of difference in Russell's pocket — and a complete loss of negotiating leverage.**

## Why This Is Not Hypothetical

Three reasons this is a live risk, not a theoretical one.

**First, Chasen is already oriented toward a sale.** The system inferred this from behavioral pattern (deferred operating decisions, the deck framings that quietly admit new sales is value-destroying, the WS-12 Holdco principal intro on the calendar since May 12). Whether or not Chasen frames it explicitly, the operating motion is converging on M&A as the exit. The question is not *whether* a sale happens; it is *when*, at *what price*, and to *whom*.

**Second, Class B has structural reasons to prefer a lower-price faster exit over a higher-price slower one.** Class B holds 66.22% of sale proceeds. At $30M, they net ~$0 (after debt + Bonus + wind-up). At $100M, they net ~$10M after the waterfall. At first glance, $100M is obviously better. But: Class B has been holding this position since October 2025; they've absorbed operational risk, regulatory risk (Barclays' BHC exposure), and reputational risk; if they believe the right strategic buyer can be found in 4 months at $30-40M with low execution risk, vs. 12-18 months at $80-100M with high execution risk and a deteriorating asset, the IRR math may favor the faster exit. And the drag-along expiration gives them the option to take the faster path without minority blockers.

**Third, Barclays has its own asymmetric exits.** Section 11.9 lets Barclays surrender all equity for $1 and walk away from the equity governance entirely. They keep the $31.4M debt claim. If Barclays exercises that option (likely if they decide Class is a regulatory or reputational liability), they no longer have an incentive to wait for a premium sale — they just want their debt paid. A $36.5M sale clears their debt. They would lean on Class B to take the first credible offer that clears the senior, regardless of equity outcomes. Russell's $1.5M of incremental upside is invisible to a creditor-only counterparty.

**Combined, the system thinks the probability of a sub-$100M sale being attempted is non-trivial.** Maybe 25-30% in the 90 days immediately after October 8. The drag-along cliff doesn't *cause* a low-ball sale; it *removes Russell's protection against one*.
