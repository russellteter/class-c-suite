## Line-by-line breakdown of the proposed terms

### 1. Title — Chief Operating Officer

The title is "Chief Operating Officer," not "Acting COO" or "Interim COO." Reporting to the CEO and the Board.

### 2. Base salary — $280,000

Rationale:
- Current base ($225K) was set for SVP, Operations and Business Development.
- The COO role at Class carries materially more accountability — direct ownership of cost reduction, operational execution, and the cash runway extension plan.
- Median base for a privately-held SaaS COO in 2026 is in the $275K–$370K range based on published market data.
- Most COO compensation packages at this stage include a meaningful equity component that compensates for lower-than-market cash. Russell's existing equity stack (180 Class E Common Units + 2.25% MIP) only pays out on a Deemed Liquidation Event under the Oct 8, 2025 Newco agreements. Given Class' current trajectory, the probability-weighted value of that equity is functionally zero.
- Higher base salary is the mechanism by which a market-comparable COO package can be delivered without restructuring the existing equity grants (which would require Barclays consent and is not proposed here).

### 3. Variable target — $100,000

Held at current level. No change in target amount.

### 4. Variable floor (Y1) — 50% = $50,000 guaranteed

Rationale:
- A 50% floor on the variable target guarantees $50,000 of variable bonus in Y1 regardless of company-level performance.
- A guaranteed floor is standard practice when an executive is being asked to step into a turnaround / cash-crisis scenario where company-wide performance metrics are unpredictable.
- The remaining 50% of the variable target ($50,000) remains performance-contingent, preserving alignment with company outcomes.

### 5. Variable payment timing — semi-annual, every 6 months

Rationale:
- Through Q4 2025, Russell received quarterly bonus payments at SVP-level comp.
- Effective Q1 2026, the variable bonus structure shifted to an annual payout, scheduled for Feb/March 2027. This created a 14+ month gap between bonus payments at a time when Russell is being asked to take on significantly more responsibility.
- Semi-annual payment (every 6 months) restores a portion of the cash flow cadence Russell had under prior structure, without requiring a full return to quarterly payment.

### 6. Sign-on / promotion bonus — $25,000 paid on COO appointment

Rationale:
- Effectively functions as a partial mid-year bonus payment to bridge the cash flow gap created when the variable structure shifted from quarterly to annual.
- Recognizes the COO step-up event as distinct from the ongoing variable bonus structure.
- Subject to forfeiture only if Russell voluntarily resigns within 12 months of appointment for reasons other than Good Reason. Not subject to repayment in any involuntary termination scenario.

### 7. Performance milestone bonus — $50,000

Trigger: Class achieves two consecutive fiscal quarters of (i) EBITDA positive AND (ii) operating cash flow positive.

Rationale:
- Single-payment, milestone-based bonus tied directly to the COO mission: restoring Class to financial sustainability.
- Both metrics are required (not "or") to ensure the bonus pays only on genuine financial recovery, not on accounting timing effects that can move EBITDA without underlying cash improvement.
- Paid as a one-time lump sum upon achievement of the trigger condition.

### 8. Severance — length: 5 months base + variable share

Rationale:
- Increased from current 4-month commitment to 5 months, reflecting the increased seniority of the COO role.
- "Variable share" means the pro-rata portion of the variable target bonus that would be earned across the 5-month period (= 5/12 × $100K = $41,667).
- Total cash severance at 5 months = $116,667 base + $41,667 variable = $158,333.

### 9. Severance — payment timing: full lump within 14 days of termination

Rationale:
- Severance is paid as a single lump-sum wire transfer within 14 days of the termination date, rather than as monthly installments over 5 months.
- Same total amount as installment payment; only the timing changes.
- Protects against the scenario in which the company becomes financially distressed or files for bankruptcy after Russell's termination but before all installment payments have been made. Lump payment within 14 days substantially eliminates this risk window.

### 10. Severance — deemed termination triggers: three events

If any of the following events occur during Russell's employment, Russell shall be deemed to have been terminated by the Company without Cause as of the date of the event, and the full severance (including the 14-day lump payment in Section 9) shall become immediately payable:

**(a) Missed payroll for more than 7 days.** If the Company fails to make a scheduled payroll payment to its employees within 7 days of the scheduled payroll date, this trigger fires.

Rationale: Missed payroll is the earliest objective indicator that the company cannot meet its current obligations. In a cash-crisis scenario, this trigger typically fires days-to-weeks before a bankruptcy filing. Including this as a deemed termination trigger ensures Russell's severance becomes payable before the company's remaining cash is exhausted or restricted by creditor action.

**(b) Bankruptcy filing.** If the Company files for protection under Chapter 7 or Chapter 11 of the U.S. Bankruptcy Code, this trigger fires.

Rationale: Once a bankruptcy filing is made, post-petition severance for insiders is severely limited by Section 503(c) of the Bankruptcy Code (capped at 10× the mean severance paid to non-management employees in the same year), and pre-petition severance claims become unsecured creditor claims subject to the §507(a)(4) wage-priority cap (~$15K per claim). A pre-filing deemed termination trigger converts what would otherwise be a low-priority bankruptcy claim into an obligation that becomes payable before the petition is filed.

**(c) Formal wind-down announcement.** If the Board formally announces a wind-down, dissolution, or cessation of operations of the Company, this trigger fires.

Rationale: Covers the orderly-shutdown scenario in which the Board decides to wind the company down rather than file for bankruptcy. Ensures Russell's severance is treated as part of the wind-down obligations rather than dependent on a residual operating decision after the announcement.
