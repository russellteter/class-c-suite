## The single most important insight from the research

**A rabbi trust does NOT protect you from company shutdown.** The most common pre-funded severance mechanism — putting cash in a rabbi trust — leaves the trust assets subject to the company's creditors in bankruptcy. So if Class runs out of cash and files Chapter 7 (or just dissolves), severance held in a rabbi trust becomes an unsecured claim alongside every other unpaid vendor. You'd be at the back of the line.

The only mechanisms that genuinely protect you against "company runs out of cash" are:
1. **Cash already in your bank account** (sign-on, prepaid retention, quarterly stay bonuses paid as earned).
2. **Severance backed by a third-party letter of credit** (a bank issues the LoC; the bank pays even if Class is dead).
3. **Severance backed by an irrevocable trust funded with assets the company cannot reach** — far harder to negotiate and structure than the first two.

If Class files Chapter 11, your severance gets even worse: **pre-petition severance is capped at the §507(a)(4) wage-priority cap (~$15K)** unless you can argue post-petition admin expense, and §503(c) caps insider retention/severance at 10× the mean severance paid to non-management employees. Translation: in bankruptcy, your contractual severance is functionally vaporized.

**This means the protection you want is NOT "more weeks of severance"** — that's the wrong fight. The fight is: **how much cash gets into your bank account before the company can't pay anymore.**

## Component menu — organized by what each piece actually does

### I. CASH CORE (the table stakes)

| Component | Description | Why it matters for you |
|---|---|---|
| **Base salary** | Annual W2 cash | Anchors all other comp; sets Good Reason floor |
| **Variable / target bonus** | Annual cash incentive on KPIs | Should be tied to metrics YOU control as COO, not company-wide ARR |
| **Sign-on / promotion cash bonus** | One-time cash on appointment | **High EV in your scenario** — money in your pocket day one, no shutdown risk |

**Recommended ask on this layer:**
- Base: **$275K–$300K** (anchored to "Chief" band per the CSV precedent)
- Variable target: **$125K–$150K** (75% on COO-controllable metrics — cost reduction milestones, gross margin, cash runway extension; 25% on company performance)
- **Promotion cash bonus: $50K–$100K paid on signing** — frame as "moving from SVP to COO is a step function" — and structure as forfeitable on voluntary resignation within 12 months without Good Reason, but NOT clawbackable on involuntary termination

### II. VARIABLE BONUS STRUCTURE (the part most execs get wrong)

This is where you build in protection without it looking like protection.

| Component | Description | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Guaranteed Y1 bonus floor (50–100%)** | Minimum % of target bonus paid regardless of attainment | In a turnaround, hitting targets is unpredictable — a 50–75% floor neutralizes plan-failure risk |
| **Pro-rata bonus on involuntary termination** | If terminated without Cause or for Good Reason mid-year, earned bonus is paid pro-rata | Standard exec ask — avoids "they fire you in Nov to skip the bonus" |
| **Bonus pre-payment / quarterly bonus** | Bonus paid quarterly rather than annually | Money in your account faster — better for the runway-end scenario |
| **Stretch / kicker bonus** | Above-target bonus for over-achievement (e.g., 150–200% of target if specific milestones hit) | Less important than the floor in your situation but worth including |
| **Cash-conservation bonus** | A specific bonus tied to extending runway by N months or reducing burn by $X | Aligns your incentive with the actual mission; harder for the board to refuse |

**Recommended ask on this layer:**
- 75% guaranteed Y1 bonus floor (= guaranteed $94K–$112K on top of base)
- Quarterly bonus payments
- Add a discrete **"Survival Bonus"**: an extra $100–$150K paid in tranches if you successfully extend runway past specific dates (e.g., $50K on hitting 12 months, $50K on hitting 18 months, $50K on hitting positive cash flow)

### III. STAY / RETENTION BONUSES (the part with the most leverage)

This is your single highest-EV ask given the cash-runway risk. The structure matters more than the size.

| Structure | How it works | Your risk profile |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-paid retention (best for you)** | Full bonus paid in cash upfront; subject to clawback if you voluntarily leave before milestone | Money in your account NOW. Clawback only applies to voluntary resignation. If Class shuts down, you keep the cash. |
| **Periodic stay bonus** | Fixed amount paid every N months you remain | E.g., $50K every 3 months. Each payment de-risks the next quarter. |
| **Milestone retention** | Lump sum paid on hitting a specific date or event | Higher risk — single payment date means single point of failure |
| **Performance retention (KEIP-style)** | Bonus tied to staying + hitting metrics | More legally defensible in distressed scenarios; less risky for the board to approve |

**Recommended structure for you:**
- **$150K–$250K pre-paid retention bonus on signing**, with this clawback schedule:
  - 100% clawback if you voluntarily resign within 6 months (other than for Good Reason)
  - 50% clawback if you voluntarily resign 6–12 months in
  - 0% clawback after 12 months
  - **0% clawback if (a) you are terminated without Cause, (b) you resign for Good Reason, (c) the company is wound down, sold, or files for bankruptcy, OR (d) the company materially fails to pay you for >30 days**
- **PLUS quarterly stay bonus** of $25K–$50K paid at the end of each quarter you remain
- Frame the pre-paid as a "sign-on bonus on the COO appointment" — it's psychologically easier for Chasen/board to approve than "retention"

**Why this matters:** A $250K pre-paid retention + 4 × $40K quarterly = $410K in your bank account over 12 months, on top of base + variable. If Class survives, you've been paid well. If Class dies in month 13, you've extracted ~$735K cash (base + variable + sign-on + quarterly) BEFORE the shutdown.

### IV. SEVERANCE — AND HOW TO ACTUALLY SECURE IT

You've identified the right risk: a contractual 12-month severance from a company that runs out of cash = zero severance. Here's the menu of protection mechanisms, ranked by how well they actually work.

| Mechanism | How it works | Effectiveness in shutdown scenario |
|---|---|---|
| **Letter of Credit-backed severance** | Bank issues an irrevocable standby LoC for the full severance amount; LoC is your direct claim against the bank | **Best protection.** Bank pays even if Class is dead. But Barclays will almost certainly refuse this — it ties up balance sheet they're trying to recover. |
| **Severance escrow with third-party trustee** | Cash deposited with an unrelated trustee, released only on triggering event | **Strong if structured correctly.** Has to be "irrevocable" and outside the company's creditors' reach. Hard to negotiate and expensive. |
| **Springing rabbi trust** | Trust funded only on a triggering event (e.g., CIC) | **Doesn't help your shutdown scenario** — fires on sale, not on shutdown. |
| **Rabbi trust funded now** | Cash in trust under company control | **Doesn't protect against shutdown** — assets remain subject to company creditors. |
| **Lump-sum severance pre-payment** | Severance paid in advance, subject to clawback if you leave voluntarily | **Effectively the same as a retention bonus.** Money in your account now. |
| **Severance paid in installments starting immediately** | Severance accrues weekly/monthly and gets paid as earned, not in lump | **Protects partially** — if Class fails in month 6 of payout, you got 6 months of severance before the bankruptcy admin-expense cap kicks in. |
| **Standard 12-month severance, post-termination** | Class pays severance from operating cash after you leave | **Zero protection.** If Class can't pay, you're unsecured. |
| **§507(a)(4) wage-priority claim in bankruptcy** | Federal Bankruptcy Code gives unpaid pre-petition severance some priority | **Effectively zero — capped at ~$15K per claim.** |

**Recommended severance package for you:**

1. **Length: 9–12 months** (from current 4 months) — but you almost certainly cannot get an LoC against this, so the contractual length matters less than the structure.

2. **Severance ACCRUAL during employment** — negotiate that severance pay accrues monthly during employment (e.g., 1 month of severance accrued per month worked, capped at 12 months) and is treated as deferred earned wages. Earned wages get a higher payment priority than discretionary severance.

3. **Severance installments start within 7 days of termination** — push for the FIRST 3 months to be paid as a lump within a week of termination, not "over 12 months." That gets cash out fast before any bankruptcy filing can claw it back.

4. **Walk-away severance trigger on company failure** — negotiate a "deemed termination without Cause" clause that triggers if the company misses payroll, files for bankruptcy, defaults on Barclays, or announces a wind-down. This converts what would be an unsecured claim into accelerated severance owed today.

5. **Severance for ANY termination not for Cause** — including "if Russell elects to resign at any point in the 6 months following the appointment, severance is paid in full." This protects you against a "this is worse than I thought" scenario after you've already committed.

6. **Acknowledge the cash reality** — you can ask for severance to be funded in escrow, but expect Barclays to block it. Use the rejection as leverage to push other items (bigger sign-on, more stay bonus).

### V. TITLE, ROLE, AND CAREER PROTECTIONS

This is where you patch the holes in your existing Newco docs.

| Component | Description | Why you need it |
|---|---|---|
| **Expanded Good Reason** | Add: title demotion, material reduction in duties or authority, reporting line change away from CEO/Board, reduction in variable comp, reduction in equity, failure to nominate/elect to Board if applicable, relocation reduced to >35 miles | Your current Good Reason only covers base reduction + 50-mile move. If you get demoted from COO back to SVP, you currently have no recourse. |
| **Narrowed Cause** | Strike "could reasonably be expected to injure business reputation"; require Cause to be (a) felony conviction (not charge), (b) intentional and material misconduct, (c) repeated willful refusal to follow lawful Board direction (with 30-day cure), (d) gross negligence resulting in material harm. Require a super-majority Board vote AND an opportunity for you to be heard with counsel before any Cause determination. | Your existing Cause definition is the company's get-out-of-severance-free card. Narrowing it directly converts severance risk into severance certainty. |
| **Board observer or formal Board access** | Right to attend Board meetings (observer if not director), formal seat on key sub-committees | Hardens your external market position post-Class; you list "Board observer" on LinkedIn going forward |
| **Title clarity** | "Chief Operating Officer" (full title, not "Acting COO" or "Interim COO"); reporting to CEO and Board directly | Avoid title hedging — once you take the role, you carry the title for life. Recruiters check title at last day of employment. |
| **Public announcement and external rights** | The company will publicly announce your promotion to COO via press release and update LinkedIn; you have the right to use the title externally even after departure | "I was COO of Class Technologies from May 2026 to [date]" — that's career-defining whether Class survives or not |
| **Vesting acceleration on company failure** | All Class E units and MIP percentage vest immediately on wind-down, asset sale to lender, or Chapter 7 filing — not just Deemed Liquidation Event | Patches the biggest gap in your equity stack |
| **Indemnification agreement** | Separate written agreement guaranteeing the company indemnifies you for any acts/omissions in your role to the maximum extent permitted by Delaware law, with an obligation to advance fees | Critical when joining a troubled company — claims against execs can come years later |
| **D&O tail coverage** | Company agrees to maintain (or fund pre-paid) tail D&O insurance for 6 yrs post-departure with Side A coverage | Side A pays you directly if the company can't indemnify (e.g., bankruptcy). Have the company pre-pay this so it survives the shutdown. |

**Why this whole section matters:** Even if Class dies in 12 months, you walk out with the COO title, a clean cause record, a tail D&O policy that protects you against future claims, and a Good Reason definition that gave you optionality to leave on your own terms.

### VI. EQUITY — what to ask for despite the low value

You already have the 180 Class E units and 2.25% MIP. Adding more units doesn't materially change your equity outcome because the equity is worth ~$0 in the most likely scenarios. But:

1. **Accelerated vesting** on your existing units — instead of getting 25% in October 2026, ask for an additional 25% to cliff-vest on COO promotion. Costs the company nothing today.
2. **Refresh grant** for the COO role — frame as "the existing 180 units were granted to VP Partnerships; the COO role merits an additional grant equal to the spread between COO and VP Partnerships market equity." Maybe another 100-150 units. Negotiating room is large because the company values these at near-zero.
3. **Repricing or strike adjustment** — only relevant if there's a future recapitalization. Worth asking for the right to be made whole in any down-round structuring.
4. **Drag-along and co-sale protection** in any future capital event — protects you from being washed out by a Barclays-led restructuring.

**My read:** Don't spend much negotiating capital here. The expected value of equity is approximately zero. Use the equity discussion as "I'm taking real downside risk on the equity I already have, so cash and protections need to make up for it."

### VII. NON-CASH BENEFITS AND POST-EMPLOYMENT PROTECTIONS

| Component | Description |
|---|---|
| **COBRA / health continuation** | Company pays full health insurance premiums for 12–18 months post-termination |
| **Outplacement services** | $25K–$50K outplacement / executive search firm allowance on termination |
| **Legal fee reimbursement** | Company pays your legal fees for negotiating this employment agreement (up to a cap, e.g., $25K) — standard in exec deals |
| **No non-compete (or narrow non-compete with garden leave)** | If non-compete required, require company to pay full base + benefits during the non-compete period — converts non-compete into paid garden leave |
| **Mutual non-disparagement** | You don't bash them; they don't bash you. Critical for your next gig. |
| **Reference language pre-agreed** | Pre-agreed positive reference letter signed at signing, held in escrow, released on departure regardless of reason (other than Cause) |
| **Communications control** | Right to review and approve internal/external communications about your departure |
| **Reimbursement for unused vacation** | Pay out at termination; some states require this anyway |

## How to think about prioritization given your "company likely dies in 12 months" base case

Rank your asks by **cash-in-hand before the cliff** rather than by **total package size**.

**High priority (push hardest):**
1. Large pre-paid sign-on / promotion bonus ($150–250K)
2. Quarterly stay bonuses ($25–50K every 90 days)
3. Severance acceleration trigger on company failure / missed payroll / bankruptcy filing
4. Expanded Good Reason
5. Narrowed Cause
6. Pre-paid D&O tail (Side A)
7. Indemnification agreement

**Medium priority:**
8. Guaranteed Y1 bonus floor (50–75%)
9. Survival/cash-conservation bonus tied to runway milestones
10. Vesting acceleration on company failure (not just Deemed Liquidation Event)
11. Title + reporting clarity
12. 9–12 month severance (longer is nice but the structure matters more)
13. Bonus pro-rata on involuntary termination

**Lower priority (negotiate but don't burn capital):**
14. Higher base ($275–300K) — yes, but pre-paid cash is more valuable in your scenario
15. Higher variable target ($125–150K)
16. Additional equity grant
17. COBRA continuation
18. Outplacement allowance
19. Legal fee reimbursement
20. Garden leave / non-compete buyout

1. **What does Brian Bharwani's package look like as CFO?** Same base ($225K) is on the CSV but his variable structure, severance, and any retention agreements he's already signed since the Newco recap are your closest internal precedent.
2. **What did Irina actually get as COO?** Still the missing data point. Her old change forms in DocuSign archive.
3. **What did Chasen and the board do to incent themselves to stick around?** Chasen presumably has a meaningful retention package separate from the MIP. That's your "if Chasen got it, I get it" leverage.
4. **What is Barclays' actual posture on management retention?** Barclays has every reason to want Russell to stay through a sale process. Frame the conversation as "I'm helping you preserve enterprise value for Barclays' recovery."
