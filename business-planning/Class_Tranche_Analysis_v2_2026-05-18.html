Why the Board Deck cash path is lower than the NS Baseline
Comparing month-to-month: Board Deck shows $2.2M at end of May vs NS Baseline at $3.0M; Board shows $1.1M at end of June vs NS at $2.2M; Board trough $0.1M in July vs NS trough $0.5M. The board deck is consistently $0.5M-$1.1M more conservative through the trough period.

Most likely reasons for the gap (not exhaustively verified):
  • More conservative collection assumptions. NS Baseline uses L12M actual collection rate; board may be using a more cautious figure given recent renewal slippage.
  • Different AWS savings ramp. Board may model AWS savings starting later than the actual GL data shows; NS Baseline reflects actual April 2026 hosting reduction.
  • Standard governance contingency. Boards typically present a downside-leaning forecast for risk management purposes; finance teams often build $200-500K of unidentified buffer.
  • Different Coso-TD treatment. Unclear if board's $3.48M starting cash includes any of the $3.245M Coso-TD pool; my driver model excludes it entirely.
  • Severance treatment. Board may already be including T1 severance ($165K) in its forecast.
Note: I cannot fully reconcile to the board's methodology without seeing the underlying model. The gap is directionally consistent with a conservative buffer of $500K-$1M, which is standard practice.

Qualitative Operational Considerations — Not In The Cash Math Above

Each tranche removes operational capability that the cash model above does NOT quantify

These items are shown separately because they're inherently estimates, not verifiable cash flows. Per your direction, they're presented for context with rough cash ranges (approximate, not modeled into the scenarios).
T1 — Sales & Marketing reduction (7 → 4)
Cuts new-logo generation capability roughly in half. The team is already lean (down from 16 sales + 9 marketing peak). With 4 remaining, the channel/partner motion likely continues, but direct outbound new-logo activity is materially reduced. Existing pipeline ($4.6M FY26) may still convert but new pipeline generation slows.
Approx revenue impact: -$100K to -$300K of new ARR over 12 months (modeled estimate, wide range)
T2 — Czech Product Dev reduction (15 → 10)
Slows product roadmap velocity. Class is a software company; product development capacity directly affects ability to retain customers when they ask for new features and to respond to competitive pressure. The Czech team is the bulk of engineering capability. Impact lags 6-18 months — customers may not feel it immediately, but renewal conversations in late 2026 / 2027 could be harder.
Approx revenue/retention impact: 1-2 percentage points of incremental churn over 12-24 months (-$200K to -$800K ARR risk)
T3 — G&A + CS + Consulting (-4 heads)
G&A is already at 6 (down from 9). Further cuts risk degraded financial reporting accuracy, compliance gaps, slower vendor management, and reduced HR capacity during a period that will likely involve more cuts/transitions. Customer success at 3 → 2 reduces touch density for at-risk accounts during the heavy Q3 renewal window.
Approx revenue/cost impact: -$100K to -$400K in retention plus elevated compliance/audit risk (hard to quantify)
T4 — Eliminate new sales (Sales → 0, Mktg → 0)
Caps the company's growth permanently. No new logo acquisition capacity. The deck itself describes this as "putting a shelf life on the company." Materially reduces M&A optionality — most acquirers want some demonstrated new-sales motion. Once executed, rebuilding the sales/marketing function takes 12-18 months and costs more than was saved.
Approx revenue impact: -$500K to -$1.2M of forgone new ARR per year, plus reduced acquisition multiple
Compounding effect across tranches
Each tranche interacts negatively with the others. Cutting sales AND product AND G&A simultaneously creates execution risk on the renewals that ARE happening. The remaining team is asked to absorb more work during the most cash-sensitive period of the year (Q3 heavy renewals).
Approx incremental risk: amplifies each tranche's individual revenue impact by 20-40%
Reversibility & speed-to-rebuild
Sales/marketing motion rebuilds in 12-18 months; product roadmap recovery takes 6-12 months; G&A rebuilds in 3-6 months. None of these are instant. If the company decides to pursue growth again later (after an M&A injection, refinance, or market shift), the cuts made today represent operational debt that takes time and cash to repay.
Approx cost to rebuild any function: ~1.5-2× the savings achieved by cutting it
Built-In Model Assumptions — Full Transparency
Everything the model does that isn't a user input
To prevent skepticism about "hidden assumptions," here is the full list of what the model bakes in beyond your editable inputs:
  1. Inflows are constant across all scenarios. Tranches affect outflows only. Modeled customer collections, renewal cohorts, and new sales cash are identical in all six scenarios.
  2. Monthly seasonality applied to subscription collections. May-Jul: 0.85-0.95×. Aug-Jan: 1.05-1.15× (heavy renewal months). Feb-Apr: 0.90-1.00×. Sourced from L6M NS observed pattern.
  3. July 4 cohort cash distributed Jul/Aug/Sep. $3.8M billed × 80% retention = $3.04M split: 16% July, 59% August, 25% September (based on typical net-30 to net-60 collection lag).
  4. Q3 cohort cash distributed Aug/Sep/Oct. $2.3M assumed cohort × 80% = $1.84M split: 27% Aug, 44% Sep, 29% Oct.
  5. Jan 2027 cohort distributed Jan-May 2027. $7M assumed × 80% = $5.6M spread per typical historical pattern.
  6. Compensation reduction = full burdened cost. Each cut head removes $20K/mo burdened comp (US) or $9K/mo (Czech) from monthly outflows, starting at the tranche's savings start month.
  7. Other operating costs held constant across scenarios. Hosting, software, licensing, reseller fees, travel, legal, insurance, cash interest are identical across all tranche scenarios (only headcount portion varies).
  8. Cash interest constant at $240K/mo. No refinance, no rate changes modeled.
  9. No working capital changes modeled. AR collection rate, AP aging, deferred revenue movement assumed neutral.
  10. No restricted cash release modeled. Coso-TD Bank ($3.245M) and Barclays BACA ($2.5M) excluded entirely from operating cash.
  11. No FX impact modeled. International receipts at constant USD equivalents.
  12. No tax payments modeled. Assumes neutral cash impact on quarterly estimated tax.
  13. Operating costs do not grow over 12 months. No inflation, no salary increases, no software repricing.
If any of these is meaningfully different in reality, the scenario outputs will shift accordingly. The relative ranking of scenarios is robust to these assumptions; the absolute cash levels are not.
