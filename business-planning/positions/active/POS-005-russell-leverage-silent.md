## Claim
Russell's negotiation leverage with Chasen depends on never voicing it. The leverage exists structurally because Chasen has no replacement COO candidate and Russell's exit would compound the cash crisis. Spoken aloud, the leverage becomes a threat, which triggers Cause-clause review (broad definition + Barclays as third-party beneficiary). The play is silent walk-away credibility, maintained through the parallel job-hunt track (WS-11), expressed only through behavior and timing, never through words.

## Evidence
- Newco LLC Agreement (2025-10-08): Good Reason is narrow; Cause is broad; Barclays is third-party beneficiary on the Cause clause
- 180 Class E units + 2.25% MIP capped ~$675K, pays only on Deemed Liquidation Event
- Russell's $0 equity value in shutdown scenario creates real walk-away credibility
- External alternatives in $325-400K range (per Apply campaign tracker)
- Memory `coo_negotiation_leverage.md` — doctrine codified

## Dependencies
- None on other positions; this is a behavioral doctrine

## Counter-evidence (would force revision)
- Chasen identifies and onboards a viable replacement COO candidate
- Cash crisis resolves and exit timing becomes optional
- Russell's external alternatives narrow materially
- A board member or Holdco principal independently raises Russell's replaceability

## Operating implication
Every comp negotiation move follows from this. Never speak the leverage. Never threaten. Behave as someone who could walk but isn't walking yet. The parallel job-hunt track is the silent backbone — it stays active in WS-11 with the Apply daily briefing skill, and that track's credibility is what makes silence powerful.

In every interaction with Chasen, default to collaborative posture, name the decision-rights owner (which is him on comp), present three crisp options, and let timing communicate what words cannot.
