## Claim
Reducing GTM headcount cannot meaningfully improve the July 26 W30 cash trough of $111,766. CFO spread-mode severance policy (2-12 weeks across normal payroll, not lump-sum) means savings begin *after* the trough, not before it. Headcount cuts are an FY27 question, not a July 26 lever.

## Evidence
- Cash Lever Model v5, sheet `04_Severance_Timing` — per-person severance payout schedules show cumulative payroll outflow over the severance period exceeds normal payroll for the cut weeks.
- CFO severance policy memo (2025-11) — spread-mode is policy, requires counsel sign-off to deviate.
- Czech statutory notice (5 months) — binding on EU staff; cannot be accelerated.
- W30 dated 2026-07-26 — sits inside the severance payout window for any cut announced after ~April 2026.

## Dependencies
- POS-006 (CFO spread-mode severance is legally required, not preference)

## Counter-evidence (would force revision)
- Counsel approves a one-time lump-sum exception for a small cohort
- Cuts announced before March 2026 (already past)
- Hiring freeze counted as in-period savings against open requisitions — but freeze savings against open reqs don't hit the W30 line directly

## Operating implication
Stop bringing headcount cuts as the answer to W30. They are a medium-term burn lever, not a near-term cash lever. See POS-003 for what actually works.
