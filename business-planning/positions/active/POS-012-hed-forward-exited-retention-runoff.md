## Claim
Class has effectively exited HED as a forward-investment segment as of 2026. Zero marketing dollars and zero demand-gen investment go to HED. Clayton Coyne handles residual inbound only. The HED renewal book ($14.7M ARR / 444 customers, 47.9% trailing concentration) remains under active account management with Emmanuel Clemot (EMEA), Nikolaos Galindo (LAC), Robert Thayer, Andee Bodenstein, Holly Hardin, and Sabina Cramer (SVP). (Petya Lolova, Armanda Sereikaite, Fiona Ong terminated 2026-05-22/2026-05-26 — accounts reassigned). The book is in managed-runoff posture — retain what we can at current rates with nominal COLA, but accept that without forward investment, the segment will contract over time. This is a coherent strategy, not a drift.

## Evidence
- Russell direct answer 2026-05-21 (Q1): "weve already effectively exited HED. No marketing dollars, investments, etc. We have Clayton Coyne handling residual inbound leads from HED, but no demand gen."
- SF open pipeline by ICP 2026-05-21: ICP HED = 29 opps / $6.5K total / $224 avg deal — micro-deals only, no real new-business motion
- SF Closed Won LTM by ICP / Type: ICP HED Renewal = 64 opps / $1.72M prior ARR → $1.63M new = −6.4% contraction
- Russell answer Q12: "exiting HED has nothing to do with exiting our account management and support of our customer base. Its about exiting GTM new business efforts and investments"
- Marketing focus is 3 named corporate ICPs + K12 Virtual Schools + ~3 corporate L&D events/year — none of which is HED
- Renewal team headcount (8 AMs) remains intact for HED retention work

## Dependencies
- Pricing model anchoring strategy holds for HED retention (POS-anchoring forthcoming)
- Renewal team retention (Emmanuel, Niko, Robert, etc.) — no flight risk through Ed exit
- Russell's COO transition does not force a "re-invest in HED" decision

## Counter-evidence (would force revision)
- Major HED competitor (Engageli, Anthology) launches at 25%+ price below Class
- Russell or Chasen decide to formally re-invest in HED demand-gen
- Either INTL renewal AM (Emmanuel or Niko) leaves within 90 days — book becomes un-coverable
- Q3 FY26 HED renewal cliff exceeds 25% gross contraction in a single quarter — managed-runoff turns into uncontrolled decline

## Operating implication
The strategy is correct because:
(a) Forward HED pipeline is generating $224 avg deals — economically meaningless;
(b) Renewal book is contracting at 6.4% naturally — gradual decline is mathematically inevitable without disproportionate investment;
(c) The 4 active ICPs (Healthcare VILT / Enterprise Learning / Training Providers / K12 Virtual Schools) have $1.7M+ open pipeline at $100K+ avg deal sizes — the forward bet is structurally better economics.

Class is not "exiting" HED as a customer base; Class is exiting HED as a *forward-growth* segment. The renewal team continues to serve, retain, and renegotiate the book. The strategic question is whether the largest HED accounts (Tier 9-11 EDU compressing to 49% GM at current rates) get repriced or managed to soft landing — that becomes the queued "Underwater + Large-HED repricing business plan" deep deliverable.
