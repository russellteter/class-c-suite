# Pass 2 — CMO Lens

**Topic:** Class organizational institutional read for WS-03

## Position
There is no CMO. Marketing is 3 people reporting to Ed Miller (CRO) — Kendall Woodard (Creative Director per SF title, Sr Digital Designer per roster — title divergence is itself the signal), Roxana Nabavian (Marketing, $100K FL), Dalton Mullins (Field Marketing Manager / SDR — also title divergence, and he's running 40,878 SF tasks in 90 days which is automation/sequence work, not field marketing). The brand voice during this cash crisis sits with Kendall by default. The "AI-native repositioning" workstream (WS-08) has no marketing executive owner. The external comms posture to customers mid-renewal and to employees during the redesign has no orchestration — it falls on Russell + Chasen ad hoc. This is how brand drift happens: not via bad creative, but via no one owning narrative integrity when the company is under stress.

## Top 3 risks (CMO lens)
1. **Mid-renewal customers will perceive instability if internal turnover signals reach them.** Class has 1,607 Former Customers in SF — meaning recent churn signal is high. Any of the 837 current customers in the at-risk renewal cohort who hear about an org redesign before it lands as a story will accelerate exit. The redesign comms plan must precede the redesign action by at least one week, and the story must be "consolidation around the customer," not "right-sizing."
2. **The "Creative Director in Brand and Customer Marketing" title for Kendall suggests she WAS or SHOULD BE running brand — but reports to Ed Miller, a CRO whose incentive is pipeline coverage, not brand integrity.** Marketing's reporting line into the CRO function is normal at growth-stage SaaS; it is dangerous in turnaround mode because the messaging Class needs is "we are stabilizing and reinvesting in your success," not "we are closing deals." Reporting line is misaligned to the operating phase.
3. **Roxana ($100K FL) and Kendall ($120K FL) functional overlap is undiagnosed.** Both titled "Marketing." If Kendall is brand/creative and Roxana is demand-gen/operations, that's fine. If they overlap, one of them is structurally redundant. Russell needs to know which before Q4.

## What this lens needs
- **CEO:** Whether a redesign comms storyline ("Russell as new COO consolidating operations around customer success") has been pre-tested with the board. If not, that's the Pass 4 deliverable for this investigation.
- **CFO:** Brand spend allocation — is there a marketing budget line beyond payroll? If FB/LinkedIn/HubSpot/etc spend is meaningful, the marketing function is bigger than 3 heads.
- **CRO:** Whether the lead flow into Daniel's and Massimo's pipelines is actually marketing-sourced (and therefore Dalton-attributable) or whether it's primarily outbound by the Manila BPO + reps' own networks.
- **COS:** What internal-comms cadence Russell has inherited or needs to establish — all-hands, town halls, Slack updates. Currently feels ad hoc.

## Quantitative anchor
- 3 marketing heads, $312K total FL = 0.6 marketers per $1M ARR. SaaS benchmark for healthy mature: ~2 marketers per $1M ARR. Class is severely under-invested in marketing.
- 1,607 Former Customers in SF history — that's >2x current customer count (837). Churn narrative is HEAVY whether the company speaks to it or not.

## Decision-rights question
**Who owns brand voice during the redesign?** If Russell doesn't take this explicitly, Chasen will take it by default — and Chasen's instinct will be CEO-comms-as-investor-comms, not employee-and-customer-comms. The COS instinct should be: Russell owns internal comms during the redesign, Chasen owns external/investor comms, Kendall executes creative for both. Name it.
