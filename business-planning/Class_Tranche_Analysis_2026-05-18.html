Scope of this analysis
This view compares only the four expense reduction tranches proposed in the May 18, 2026 board deck against two baselines:
  • Board Deck Forecast — the cash trajectory as published on slide 16 of the deck.
  • NetSuite Status Quo Baseline — the same operating company with no tranches executed, modeled bottom-up from NS GL with current run-rate assumptions.
Other strategic levers exist (debt refinancing, M&A, wind-down) and are noted at the end as additional considerations — but they are not charted in this view. This deliverable answers one question only: what does the cash picture look like under each of the proposed expense reduction options?

Additional Considerations — Items Not Modeled In These Six Scenarios

The scenarios above isolate cost-cutting alternatives. The full strategic picture also includes the following levers — quantified here for reference but intentionally not added to the cash chart above.

1
Debt refinancing. Cash interest run-rate from NS GL Acct 6750 is $240K/month, validated over 6 months. Annualized cash interest = $2.88M. If converted to PIK (added to principal), the cash savings would be $2.88M/year. For reference: T1 + T2 + T3 + T4 combined = $4.02M annualized; refinancing alone delivers 72% of that figure with zero operational change. Requires Barclays consent.
2
Restricted cash pools. $2.5M sits in Barclays BACA (NS Acct 1104) and $3.245M in Coso-TD Bank (NS Acct 1107). Neither is in the operating cash above. Access mechanics, release schedules, and lender consent requirements for these pools are unresolved. Either being partially available could move the July trough by $1-2M.
3
M&A transaction. Board deck includes an "Update from Michael" placeholder slide. A closed deal in Q3 2026 would deliver lump-sum liquidity that no operating scenario can match; a deal failure would force more aggressive cost action. The model above does NOT incorporate either path.
4
Working capital optimization. $1.3M is currently overdue to Anthology from June 2025. $23.3M of invoices billed in January 2026 produced only $5.2M of cash through April — implying ~$18M in slow-paying AR or invoicing-to-revenue-recognition gap. AR aging shows $660K of receivables over 180 days, including $1.4M from BME Saudi from 2022-2023 against a $338K bad debt reserve. Working capital cleanup could surface $1-2M of one-time cash.
5
Operational risk of each tranche (not in cash math). The cash analysis above is gross of any second-order revenue impact. The board deck does not quantify operational damage. For context: Class is at 41 heads against $20.6M ARR ($502K rev/employee — above SaaS healthy benchmark of $200-400K). Headcount is down 52% from December 2024 peak (85 → 41). Sales is already at 4 heads (down from 16); Marketing at 3 (down from 9); Renewals at 6 (down from 8); G&A at 6 (down from 9). All Tranches removes another 15 heads.
6
Renewal rate sensitivity. All scenarios above assume 80% renewal rate per board deck. The model is highly sensitive to this: each 1pp drop in renewal rate translates to roughly $200K of cash variance in the 12-month forecast. A 5pp drop (to 75%) would impact cash by ~$1M, exceeding the cash benefit of Tranches 2+3+4 combined.
7
Timing risk on Tranche 2 (Czech). Czech labor law requires a 5-month notice period. If executed today (May 18), cash severance ($475K) flows May-October 2026; savings begin October. The trough month (July 2026) sees severance impact but no offsetting savings. Tranche 2's severance is paid during the cash-tightest window.
8
Interest on the savings spread. All tranches reduce a P&L line that is already serviceable from current operations during heavy collection months (August-January). The cash benefit only "binds" during the trough period and during 2027 spring trough. Approximately 4-5 months/year are when these savings genuinely matter for cash, not 12.
